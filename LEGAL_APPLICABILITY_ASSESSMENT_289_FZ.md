# MASTER — Legal Applicability Assessment: Federal Law No. 289-FZ

**Document status:** LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED  
**Assessment date:** 2026-11-08  
**Scope:** preliminary product/legal applicability assessment for the planned MASTER platform; not a legal opinion and not proof of compliance.  
**Project state:** documentation-only foundation; no production platform implementation.

## 1. Executive assessment

This assessment separates two questions that must not be collapsed:

1. **Digital-platform owner layer:** whether MASTER is a “digital platform” and what duties apply to its owner under Federal Law No. 289-FZ, including Article 4(1). **Status: LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED.**
2. **Intermediary digital platform layer:** whether the actual MASTER product meets Article 2(9) and Government Decree No. 54 additional criteria and is included in the statutory register. **Status: NOT VERIFIED (register check); NOT EXPECTED TO MEET THRESHOLDS (current pilot estimate).**

The planned MASTER product — a website/PWA that enables an open-ended set of customers and service providers to publish requests, respond, exchange information, and potentially conclude service contracts — appears capable of meeting the broad statutory definition of a digital platform in Article 2(2). This is a preliminary functional mapping, not a final legal classification.

The planned Lesnoy pilot is not expected to approach the quantitative thresholds in Government Decree No. 54. No measured audience, seller/provider count, or eligible transaction value has been supplied, so threshold non-attainment is **not verified**. The legal classification must be reassessed against actual functionality, operator identity, measured annual data, and current legislation.

**Do not state that “289-FZ does not apply to MASTER.”** The defensible interim statement is: general digital-platform-owner applicability requires legal review; intermediary-platform status is not verified and is not expected at pilot scale.

## 4. Digital-platform owner layer — Article 2(2), (3) and Article 4(1)

### 4.1 Functional mapping

Article 2(2) defines a digital platform broadly by reference to an information system, website and/or software providing technical, organizational, informational or other capabilities for interaction among an unlimited circle of persons, including information exchange and sale of goods, performance of work, or provision of services.

Planned MASTER capabilities include:
- public service-category pages and provider profiles;
- customer requests and provider responses/offers;
- search, matching, ranking, and communications;
- agreement/order workflows and evidence of performance;
- reviews, complaints, and dispute-support processes;
- potentially payment initiation or provider-payment integrations in a later phase.

These features make the digital-platform definition a serious legal question. The planned platform is not merely a private internal tool.

### 4.2 Article 4(1) owner duties — legal review

Article 4(1) states duties for the owner of a digital platform, including equal and unimpeded access for an unlimited circle of persons, access to platform-use rules, non-discriminatory conditions for persons in the same category, publication of legally significant contact information, and compliance with applicable legal requirements.

Do not assume these duties disappear merely because MASTER is not included in the intermediary-platform register. Conversely, do not assume that every operator-specific duty in Articles 5–20 automatically applies to a platform outside the intermediary-platform status. Counsel must resolve the interaction between Article 1 scope, Article 2 definitions, and Article 4(1)–(2) for MASTER's actual operating model.

**Interim status: LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED.** This document does not authorize launch on the assumption that Article 4(1) is inapplicable.

## 5. Intermediary-platform definition and quantitative criteria

### 5.1 Functional statutory elements

Article 2(9) describes an intermediary digital platform as a digital platform that:
- facilitates interaction among an operator, partners, and users for the purpose of concluding civil-law contracts;
- provides technical capabilities for placing orders and/or product/work/service cards, making transactions, and paying for goods/work/services by the user in favor of the partner;
- meets the additional criteria established under Article 4(4); and
- is included in the intermediary digital-platform register under Article 4(2).

The exact role of each functional element — particularly whether MASTER's planned request/offer flow is a statutory “order,” whether an in-platform agreement is concluded, and what payment capability is provided — requires legal review.

### 5.2 Government Decree No. 54 — Russian owner/operator case

For a platform whose owner, or person contracted to operate it, is a Russian legal entity or Russian individual entrepreneur, Decree No. 54 establishes the additional threshold structure:

**100,000 AND (10,000 OR RUB 50 billion)**

Specifically:
- average daily number of users located in the Russian Federation and accessing the platform during the preceding calendar year: **at least 100,000**; and
- at least one of:
  - **10,000 or more persons** who completed at least one sale/work/service transaction during the preceding calendar year for which payment was made using the technical capabilities of the digital platform; or
  - aggregate value of such paid transactions of **at least RUB 50 billion** during the preceding calendar year.

The 10,000-person and RUB 50-billion indicators are based on information about such transactions obtained from fiscal-cash-register (KKT) data under Decree No. 54. The exact measurement and attribution methodology must be validated by counsel/accounting specialists and the competent authorities.

Decree No. 54 is effective from 1 October 2026 through 1 October 2032. Its thresholds must not be treated as immutable: Government amendments, replacement criteria, and changes to the governing law must be monitored.

**Foreign-owner/operator qualification:** Decree No. 54 contains a separate rule for a platform whose owner or contracted operator is a foreign legal entity, foreign organization, foreign citizen, or stateless person. Do not mechanically apply the Russian-operator formula to that case. The legal entity and operator structure for MASTER must be established before the criteria are finalized.

## 6. Register: authority, inclusion routes, and monitoring

The register is maintained by the Ministry of Economic Development under Government Decree No. 504. The rules provide two distinct routes:

1. **Authority-led identification and inclusion:** the authorized body receives information from federal executive authorities, evaluates the statutory and additional criteria, notifies the platform owner/operator of a positive decision, and places the entry in the register according to the prescribed procedure.
2. **Voluntary application:** the owner or contracted operator may submit an application for inclusion with supporting documents.

**Important legal correction:** the reviewed text of Government Decree No. 504 describes the platform owner's/operator's application route as voluntary (“вправе добровольно обратиться”). It does not support the blanket statement that the owner must itself file an application immediately upon reaching the thresholds. The rules also provide for authority-led identification using interagency information. Counsel must confirm whether any separate duty to notify, provide information, or take action arises under another provision or later amendment.

Operationally, MASTER must not rely on “the regulator will find us” or on the voluntary nature of the application route. It must maintain threshold monitoring, legal ownership of the assessment, prompt review of official notices, and a documented response/objection process. A positive decision can be challenged through the procedure and deadlines in Decree No. 504; counsel must own any response.

The register's existence and MASTER's presence/absence must be checked against the official Ministry source at each assessment. Do not use an undated count of listed platforms as evidence of MASTER's status. Public reports of “13 platforms” are dated snapshots and may be superseded by later amendments or entries.

## 7. “Paid transaction” and Direct-pay scenario analysis

Decree No. 54 uses the phrase payment “using the technical capabilities of the digital platform.” Whether a particular payment flow satisfies this element is a legal and factual question; the fact that funds do not pass through a MASTER bank account is not, by itself, decisive.

| Scenario | Preliminary treatment for threshold analysis | Required legal questions |
|---|---|---|
| MASTER only publishes provider contact details; parties arrange and pay entirely outside the platform | Potentially lower connection to platform payment capability; not a safe harbor | Does the platform still provide order/transaction/contract functionality under Article 2(9)? What counts as use of technical capabilities? |
| Customer and provider agree scope/price in MASTER; payment is made directly to provider through the provider's independent bank details | UNCERTAIN | Does in-platform order/contract plus direct payment constitute a qualifying transaction? Is the payment technically enabled or facilitated by MASTER? |
| MASTER generates or embeds a payment link, QR, checkout, or payment instruction | Higher risk that platform payment capabilities are used | Who generates/controls the link, what data returns to MASTER, and how is payment attributed? |
| MASTER integrates with an acquiring/marketplace provider, split payment, or provider payout API | Strong indication of platform payment functionality; legal analysis required | Does provider settlement count as payment using platform technical capabilities even if MASTER is not payee or custodian? |
| Payment is wholly outside MASTER, with no payment link/API/checkout or platform payment instruction | Potentially less likely to meet the payment element | Does the platform otherwise provide order/card/transaction functionality; what is the statutory meaning of payment capability? |

**Direct-pay is an operating option, not a statutory exclusion.** Even if a carefully designed Direct-pay route were found not to count as payment using the platform's technical capabilities for the Decree No. 54 transaction thresholds, it does not by itself decide the separate Article 4(1) digital-platform-owner question.

Do not design a payment or product flow for the purpose of artificially suppressing a statutory metric. Counsel must assess substance over labels, end-to-end user journey, contracts, technical integration, transaction attribution, and anti-circumvention risk. Question 15 below specifically addresses this issue.

## 10. Questions for Russian legal counsel

The written opinion must use the actual planned UX, user journeys, legal entity/operator structure, contracts, payment diagrams, data flows, and category list.

1. Does the planned MASTER website/PWA meet Article 2(2)'s digital-platform definition? Who is its owner under Article 2(3), and who would be the operator under Article 2(8) if the intermediary-platform regime applies?
2. How should Article 1's scope be read together with Article 4(1): which owner duties apply to MASTER if it is a digital platform but is not included in the intermediary-platform register?
3. Confirm each Article 1(3) and 1(4) exclusion against the final MASTER model. Are any additional statutory exclusions relevant?
4. Does the planned Request → Offer → Agreement → Order workflow meet the Article 2(9) interaction, order/card, transaction, and contract elements?
5. What is the legally correct interpretation of “payment using the technical capabilities of the digital platform” under Decree No. 54 for each Direct-pay, payment-link, QR, embedded checkout, acquiring, split-payment, and provider-payout scenario?
6. Which threshold regime applies to the actual owner/operator structure: Russian or foreign? Confirm the statutory Article 2(9) criteria and all additional Decree No. 54 criteria, including measurement periods and KKT data attribution.
7. Does MASTER have any duty to file an application, provide information, notify the authority, or otherwise act when thresholds are met? Reconcile Article 4(2), Government Decree No. 504 Sections III–IV, and any other applicable acts. Distinguish authority-led identification from voluntary application.
8. What are the consequences and deadlines after a positive authority decision, notification, proposed inclusion, register entry, or disagreement? What evidence and objection process must be ready?
9. What checks and data sources are mandatory for partner onboarding under Article 5 and Government Decree No. 768? Which are mandatory for individuals, NPD providers, IPs, and legal entities?
10. Which Article 17 standards, qualifications, safety checks, and limits apply to each planned category (electrical work, plumbing, heating, construction, appliance repair, legal/IT services, etc.)?
11. Which complaint and dispute procedures, response periods, appeal rights, and sanction-reversal duties under Article 14 apply? Confirm exact statutory time limits before encoding SLA values.
12. What information must be provided to partners and customers about orders, prices, remuneration, ranking, ratings, access restrictions, and contract terms?
13. Which data must be transmitted under Article 20 and Government Decree No. 766, by whom, on what schedule, in what format, and with what retention/security requirements?
14. Who is the personal-data operator for each processing purpose? Are MASTER and providers independent operators, processors, or joint operators in any scenario? What notices, agreements, legal bases, localization, retention, and data-subject processes are required?
15. Could any proposed product or payment design — including Direct-pay, omission of payment links, fragmented transaction routes, or keeping metrics just below a threshold — be treated as artificial circumvention? Assess actual economic substance, anti-abuse doctrines, reporting data, and regulatory risk. Do not assume a particular numerical proximity to a threshold is itself unlawful.
16. What are MASTER's duties concerning service quality, consumer claims, provider tax breaches, refunds, warranties, and the accuracy of provider-verification representations?
17. Which accounting, tax, consumer, personal-data, and evidence-retention periods apply to each record class? Identify legal holds and conflicts between deletion and statutory retention.
18. Which later amendments, government decrees, ministry orders, official interpretations, or court decisions must be included in the recurring legal-monitoring process?

## 11. Reassessment triggers and monitoring

### 11.1 Quantitative monitoring

Maintain an auditable annual metrics record for:
- average daily users located in the Russian Federation;
- number of persons with at least one qualifying paid transaction using platform technical capabilities;
- aggregate value of qualifying transactions;
- the data source, extraction date, measurement methodology, KKT/provider evidence, exclusions, deduplication rules, and responsible reviewer.

The metrics system must distinguish raw provider/KKT/source records from derived counts and aggregates. No estimate may be labelled VERIFIED without reproducible source evidence.

### 11.2 Review cadence

- Re-check the current wording of Decree No. 54 and related statutory criteria at least annually and before each annual planning cycle.
- Review official register status and Ministry of Economic Development publications at least quarterly during growth and before launch of any new payment/contract feature.
- Review thresholds immediately after any amendment to 289-FZ, Decree No. 54, Decree No. 504, or related rules.
- Reassess before enabling new regions, national marketing, high-volume campaigns, payment links, checkout, split payments, provider payouts, or new service categories.
- Reassess when any metric reaches an internal warning band set by the product owner with counsel. Warning bands are operational alerts, not statutory thresholds or legal conclusions.

Decree No. 54 currently states an end date of 1 October 2032. This is not a guarantee that the same thresholds will remain unchanged until that date.
